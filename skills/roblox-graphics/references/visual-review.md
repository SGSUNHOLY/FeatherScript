# Visual review

Keep one representative view and a small list of visible problems. Fix the highest-impact problem, capture again and compare under the same conditions. A more saturated or brighter image is not automatically better.

| Symptom | Inspect before changing | Useful adjustment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scene feels flat | Foreground/background values, shape lighting, camera angle | Separate the focal silhouette and introduce purposeful depth |
| Surfaces look plastic | Repeated roughness, oversized highlights, uniform edges | Match response and wear to the material; vary only where justified |
| Environment feels noisy | Competing contrast, repeated details, texture scale | Reduce secondary detail around the focal path |
| Effects overwhelm combat | Contact timing, opaque layers, overlap | Preserve character/attack silhouettes and bound concurrent effects |
| Atmosphere hides gameplay | Visibility at actual movement speed and quality | Reduce obstruction near routes and important targets |
| Quality falls apart on mobile | Shadows, transparencies, particles and geometry at low quality | Preserve the primary shape with fewer layers rather than removing the whole effect |

For a new style, build one representative corner or asset family first. Establish a reusable palette, texture density and lighting language before expanding across the map. Do not change an unrelated controller or rebuild the entire place to perform a graphics pass.

Use available performance observations to set budgets for the target scene. Avoid universal part, triangle or particle limits presented as measured requirements. Static pictures establish appearance only; movement, overlap and target-device checks establish behavior under play.
